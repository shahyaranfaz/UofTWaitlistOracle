      HOW THE ORACLE WORKS

      Methodology

      The Oracle estimates whether a particular waitlist rank will clear for a selected U of T lecture. It runs entirely in the browser and is not an official forecast or academic advice.

      Data and target

      The public archive provides enrollment demand, section capacity, capacity changes, and collection timestamps. Queue size is inferred as max(enrollment demand − section capacity, 0). Offerings are aligned by days before the applicable waitlist deadline.

      Each training row represents one rank in one lecture on one observed day. The row is labelled cleared when cumulative observed queue movement from that day to the deadline reaches that rank. Capacity increases count as queue movement.

      Model

      The Oracle uses separate histogram gradient-boosted tree models for Fall/Winter and Summer. Each ensemble contains 200 small trees with at most 15 leaves. Missing numeric values are filled with the training median. No post-hoc calibration is applied.

      The model uses 26 numeric features:

      • Rank: raw rank, rank divided by capacity, and rank divided by waitlist size
      • Queue: raw size, log size, and queue divided by capacity
      • Capacity: section capacity and recent capacity changes
      • Time: days remaining, squared days, and piecewise seven, fourteen, and sixty-day effects
      • Movement: observed three-day and seven-day movement plus seven-day velocity
      • Interactions: rank and queue ratios near the deadline plus an indicator for ranks above 30% of capacity
      • Context: campus and course-term indicators, including their near-deadline interactions

      The selected lecture supplies live queue, capacity, and movement inputs. A lecture identifier such as LEC0101 is not a feature.

      Understanding Driven by

      The Driven by list is a local explanation of the displayed estimate. For each feature group, the browser calculates the prediction again after replacing that group’s values with their training medians. A + means the entered case raises the estimate relative to those typical values, while a − means it lowers it. Because trees contain interactions, these effects do not need to add up to the displayed percentage and should not be interpreted as causal.

      Training and validation

      The archive covers completed sessions from 2022 through Summer 2026. Validation follows time rather than randomly mixing rows. Each fold trains on all earlier sessions and predicts the next one:

      No validation rows enter that fold's model fit, preprocessing values, or capacity-only baseline fit. Metrics are calculated per holdout and averaged across the three folds for each season. Each lecture-section day receives equal total weight so large queues do not dominate.

      After the model and feature choices are complete, the production models are refit on all completed sessions for their season. The current session is used only to build live inputs.

      Accuracy uses a 50% cutoff. Brier measures probability error. ECE measures calibration. AUC measures how well likely clears are ranked above likely misses.

      Baselines

      The historical percentage is how often the same rank cleared in earlier lectures at the equivalent date. The literal 10% rule predicts clear when rank is no more than 10% of capacity. The boosted 10% rule is a fitted probability curve that still uses only rank divided by capacity. All methods are scored on the same historical-comparable rows. That subset covers 61.9% of Fall/Winter validation weight and 53.9% of Summer validation weight.

      Production and limitations

      The exported tree splits, leaf values, and preprocessing medians run locally in the browser. Historical outcomes are fetched and displayed separately. They do not change the model percentage.

      Clearance is inferred from snapshots rather than student records, so movement between snapshots can be missed. Scheduling, reserved seats, and student behaviour can change between years. Summer has fewer comparable observations and should be treated with more caution.
